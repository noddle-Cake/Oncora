# Oncora — submission

> **These are computational research predictions, not validated clinical conclusions.**

Generated by `uv run python scripts/build_submission.py` on 2026-10-17. Every number below is read from a committed evaluation report or model card.

Oncora takes a compound as a SMILES string and returns ranked protein targets, ranked sensitive cancer cell lines, ranked cancer types, and a plain-language evidence summary in which every claim cites its source.

**Does the model learn selectivity?**

Selectivity (compound split): ranking a compound's top-decile lines, PR-AUC 0.238 (95% CI lower 0.221) vs cell-line-mean 0.192 — **better**. Ordering the full list of lines, per-compound ρ 0.270 (CI lower 0.254) vs 0.275 — not better. The compound-only ablation scores 0.101 on that PR-AUC, at the 0.100 prevalence floor, because it cannot vary across lines: the model's margin therefore comes from the cell-line expression features. **The model identifies which lines respond most to a given compound better than the line-average ranking does, but does not order the full list better.** Read the top of the list as the usable signal, not the tail.

A compound-features-only ablation reaches per-cell-line ρ 0.377 (it cannot vary across lines, so its per-compound ρ is undefined), which isolates how much of the signal is potency.

- Top predicted targets: EGFR (1.00), EPHB4 (0.96), EPHA2 (0.93).
- Held-out ChEMBL actives (never trained on): Epidermal growth factor receptor, Receptor-interacting serine/threonine-protein kinase 2, Transcription factor p65.

## Limitations (stated plainly)

1. **Constructed negatives.** Most target negatives are compounds active elsewhere but never measured on that target, assumed inactive. Some are untested actives, so measured precision is a lower bound.
2. **DSS3 mixes scales.** Every value ≥ 1 comes from 22 reference compounds measured across merged studies; they are excluded from headline training, with a rescaled sensitivity analysis reported separately.
3. **Cancer-type ranking needs an external file.** No workspace file maps cell-line IDs to diseases; the API returns cell-line rankings with an explicit note until DepMap `Model.csv` is installed.
4. **L1000 signatures are evidence only.** They are never model features, because a new SMILES has no measured signature at inference.
5. **CPU-only baselines.** No GNN or chemical language model; the bar was honest validation, not state-of-the-art.
6. **Selectivity is the hard part.** Compound identity explains roughly 70% of sensitivity variance and cell-line identity only 4–5%, so ranking lines *within* a compound is much harder than ranking compounds.
7. **Target accuracy depends on molecule size.** Measured known-target ranks out of 1,360: dasatinib 2-5 and imatinib 4-6 (33-37 heavy atoms), but aspirin 128-198 and paracetamol 368-458 (11-13 heavy atoms). ECFP features are too sparse to place small fragments, and aspirin's covalent mechanism is not representable at all. Inputs below 20 heavy atoms are flagged as outside the validated domain in the API, the evidence summary and the UI. The top-1 metric above is computed over mostly drug-like test compounds and does not capture this failure mode.
8. **The sensitivity model is not mechanistic.** In the demo, its top-ranked lines for gefitinib are EGFR-low (15th percentile) even though measured sensitivity correlates positively with EGFR expression (ρ = 0.219). Its ranking carries real but weak signal that is not explained by the predicted target (`reports/demo/commentary.md`).
